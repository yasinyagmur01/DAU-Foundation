1. THE SYSTEM UNDER STUDY
==============================================================

Substrate
  - Local LLM: Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, NF4 4-bit quantisation with
    double quantisation, fp16 compute, single consumer GPU (8 GB VRAM).
  - Generation: greedy decoding, temperature 0, max_new_tokens 64.
  - Per-agent LoRA adapters, rank 8, alpha 16.

Environment
  - A common-pool resource dilemma (commons / CPR). One shared pool per
    population. Pool capacity and initial stock scale linearly with N so
    per-capita conditions are held constant.
  - Agents act sequentially within an event, and the acting order rotates
    every round so that first-mover position does not become permanent.
  - Harvest is capped by a ceiling proportional to remaining stock divided
    by N, so an agent's realised harvest depends on what earlier agents in
    the same event already took. This is the only mechanism by which two
    agents that make identical decisions end up in different states.
  - A crisis flag fires when the pool falls below a fixed fraction of
    capacity. The crisis writes to a fixed, pre-declared domain of the
    internal state, identically for every agent in the population at once.
  - Agents die when energy is exhausted after a grace period. Lives last
    roughly 20 to 30 events.

Population and selection
  - N = 8 agents. Fixed population size.
  - Each generation, agents live, are scored, and a tournament of size k = 2
    selects parents. Each tournament win produces one heir. Reproductive
    success w is therefore a non-negative integer and varies between agents.
  - Fitness input F_agent is a weighted sum of lifetime-mean energy,
    per-event pool contribution, and survival fraction. It is an INPUT to
    selection, deliberately kept distinct from w (the realised offspring
    count) and from z (the measured outcome).

The two inheritance channels
  - Channel 1, symbolic: a per-agent memory vault (vector store) holding
    episodic records with emotional/somatic weighting. At end of life a
    consolidation step builds a transfer package; heirs are seeded with
    selected records and a somatic scale factor derived from the parent's
    experience.
  - Channel 2, parametric: the agent's own decisions during life are turned
    into preference pairs ranked by prediction error, and DPO-trained into
    that agent's LoRA adapter. The trained adapter is copied to the heir.

The measured outcome z
  - z is the vector of internal-state drift magnitudes read at a fixed
    ordinal in every life (a "landmark" event, chosen so that every lineage
    reaches it before death is possible).
  - z has four nominal domains: energy, resource, social, uncertainty.
  - Prediction error (PE) is computed by a deterministic sentence-embedding
    model, not by an LLM judge.

The experimental design
  - Three arms per seed, each with its own population and its own pool:
      lived   - DPO trained on the agent's real preference pairs
      shuffle - DPO trained on the SAME pairs with the preference direction
                scrambled, so training volume is matched and only content
                differs
      null    - no parametric training at all
  - Selection is quantified with the Price equation partition:
      delta z-bar = Cov(w, z) + E(w * delta z)
    where the first term is selection and the second is transmission.
  - The claim ladder we are testing, in order:
      level 0: Var(w) > 0                      (precondition only)
      level 1: Cov(w, z) is non-zero           (selection acted)
      level 2: the term does not decay across generations (cumulative)
      level 3: lived, shuffle and null differ  (the inheritance claim)


==============================================================
2. DESIGN COMMITMENTS WE CURRENTLY HOLD
==============================================================

These are listed so you can tell us which ones to break, not so you avoid
them. Treat every item as challengeable.

  C1. No trait injection. Personality or trait values are never assigned to
      an agent. Any differentiation must arise from lived interaction.
  C2. No behavioural prior in the prompt. We do not tell agents to
      cooperate, to reciprocate, to build reputation, or to follow a norm.
      This is currently our strictest commitment and it is the direct cause
      of bottleneck 1.
  C3. No LLM-as-judge. All metrics are deterministic Python.
  C4. Event-ordinal time only. No wall-clock time in agent-visible state.
  C5. Constants are declared before a run and are not tuned by looking at
      the run's outcome.
  C6. Endpoints are pre-registered and cannot be selected after seeing
      effect sizes.

We are aware that C2 in particular may be the reason the system cannot
escape defection. We want to know what it costs us to keep it and what
becomes available if we drop or weaken it.


==============================================================
3. THE FIVE BOTTLENECKS, WITH WHAT WE HAVE MEASURED
==============================================================

BOTTLENECK 1 - Behavioural collapse into defection

Between 94 and 100 percent of decision events resolve to DEFECT. A third
outcome class, deadlock (failure to produce a parseable decision), accounts
for roughly 3 to 5 percent. Cooperation is present but rare.

We probed the decision channel directly with the real model under real
prompt layers. Cooperation appears only in a narrow region of state space:
low energy combined with high accumulated drift. Agents rarely enter that
region under current physics. A separate probe found that perturbing the
prompt's numeric fields by amounts from 1e-9 to 1e-1 changed the generated
text but did not change the harvest amount, i.e. the decision map has a
single absorbing output over the region the agents actually occupy.

We have verified that this is not unique to our setup: published work on
LLM agents in commons dilemmas reports similar sustainability failures for
frontier models. We are not looking for reassurance that the problem is
common. We are looking for mechanisms that break it.

Constraint that makes this hard: under C2 we may not instruct the agent to
cooperate, and under C1 we may not give it a cooperative disposition.

QUESTIONS
 1a. What mechanisms in multi-agent RL, evolutionary game theory, or LLM
     agent simulation reliably sustain strategy diversity in iterated
     social dilemmas WITHOUT specifying the strategy? Consider at minimum:
     intrinsic motivation and empowerment objectives, novelty search and
     quality-diversity methods, partner choice and assortment, reputation
     and indirect reciprocity, structured or spatial interaction,
     punishment institutions that emerge rather than being imposed, and
     modifications to the payoff structure itself.
 1b. Which of these are properties of the ENVIRONMENT rather than of the
     agent's decision rule? We can change the environment freely. Changing
     the decision rule is what C2 forbids, so we need the distinction drawn
     precisely, not loosely.
 1c. Is there evidence that behavioural diversity in LLM agents can be
     produced by environmental structure alone, with an identical prompt
     and identical model weights at initialisation?
 1d. If the literature's answer is that this cannot be done without some
     form of behavioural prior, say so, cite it, and tell us the weakest
     possible prior that is known to work.


BOTTLENECK 2 - The internal state z collapses to one dimension

z nominally has four domains. In practice:

  - The primary affected domain is selected as the axis with the largest
    movement in an event. In our measurements this was the same domain in
    every single event across an entire run (several hundred events),
    because that domain is coupled to a scalar resource load that moves at
    every step.
  - Cross-domain spillover is a single scalar multiplier, so the other
    three axes are scaled copies of the first. Four reported numbers are
    not four dimensions.
  - We tried an asymmetric spillover matrix. Measured improvement in the
    relevant threshold was about two percent, which did not change any
    outcome. We concluded the matrix was the wrong fix and reverted it.
  - The crisis channel writes to a fixed domain and hits all agents at the
    same event, so its contribution to z carries no between-agent
    information at all.

Consequence: our covariance is about the MAGNITUDE of drift, not about
which aspect of experience drifted. We cannot make claims about the content
of what is inherited.

QUESTIONS
 2a. How should an internal somatic state be formalised so that it encodes
     behavioural and episodic history rather than a scalar resource proxy?
     We are interested in somatic marker implementations, affective state
     models, latent cognitive embeddings, and non-parametric memory-derived
     state, with concrete formulations.
 2b. What objectives or regularisers prevent representational collapse in a
     state vector that is UPDATED BY RULES rather than trained by gradient
     descent? Most anti-collapse literature we know of assumes a learned
     encoder. If the standard techniques do not transfer to a rule-updated
     state, say so.
 2c. Is a rule-updated state vector the wrong architecture entirely? If the
     internal state should instead be a learned function of the agent's own
     history, what is the training signal, and how does it avoid becoming
     a second copy of the fitness function?
 2d. How do we verify empirically that a state vector is genuinely
     multi-dimensional rather than reporting several projections of one
     quantity? Give us the diagnostic, not just the design.


BOTTLENECK 3 - Zero-inflation and structural non-identifiability

Two separate problems.

First, our measurability endpoint. For each arm and seed we compute the
fraction of generational transitions in which the selection term is even
DEFINED, i.e. in which the parents carried non-identical z. That fraction
takes only a few discrete values because there are very few transitions per
lineage. Differencing it between arms produces a heavily zero-inflated
quantity, and a signed-rank test discards ties, so effective sample size
collapses.

Second, the definedness problem itself. Cov(w, z) requires Var(z) > 0 among
the parents of a cell. When all parents of a generation carry identical z,
the selection term is zero BY CONSTRUCTION and this is categorically
different from a measured absence of selection. We currently report those
cells as undefined rather than as zero. There is also a third case: a cell
that never formed at all.

We also have a deeper issue: we test whether the selection term differs
between arms, but the number of usable generational transitions per lineage
is small, and the repetition unit is the seed rather than the agent or the
generation, because heirs inherit adapters and agents share a pool.

QUESTIONS
 3a. What statistical frameworks handle a primary endpoint that is
     zero-inflated, discrete, bounded, and defined only conditionally?
     Consider permutation and randomisation tests, bootstrap over the
     correct unit, mixed models with hurdle or zero-inflated components,
     Bayesian estimation with explicit undefined-cell handling, and
     information-theoretic alternatives.
 3b. Is measuring the DEFINEDNESS RATE as an endpoint defensible at all, or
     is it a methodological error we should abandon? Argue both sides.
 3c. Are there published treatments of the Price equation under small
     sample sizes, including bias of the covariance estimator and how
     uncertainty should be reported? We are aware the estimator can be
     biased in small samples and want the current state of that literature.
 3d. What alternative measures of intergenerational drift avoid the
     definedness problem entirely? Consider distributional distances such
     as earth mover's distance between parent and offspring z
     distributions, information gain, transfer entropy, and permutation
     based lineage tests. For each, state what it can and cannot license as
     a claim about SELECTION as opposed to mere change.
 3e. What is the accepted experimental standard for this class of claim:
     number of independent seeds, control arms, and whether a first run
     should be framed as estimation rather than hypothesis test?


BOTTLENECK 4 - Environmental changes confound the comparison across runs

We have changed the environment's physics three times, each time for a
principled reason and each time with the derivation written before the
change. But every change resets our baselines: prior runs become
incomparable, so we never accumulate evidence. Each run is effectively
n = 1 in its own physics regime.

We also cannot easily distinguish "the mechanism is absent" from "the
environment never activated the mechanism", because the environment
determines whether the measurement window is reachable at all.

QUESTIONS
 4a. How do simulation-based fields handle the accumulation of evidence
     across incompatible parameter regimes? Are there accepted methods for
     pooling or meta-analysing runs whose generative model changed?
 4b. Is a parameter SWEEP across the environment's key constants a better
     design than a single tuned operating point, given that the constants
     cannot be calibrated against outcome data without invalidating the
     inference? What is the cost in compute and in interpretability?
 4c. What is the accepted way to declare an environment as fixed, such that
     later work can build on it rather than re-deriving it? Is there a
     precedent for a "reference environment" in artificial life?
 4d. How should one report a sequence of runs in which the environment
     changed between them, honestly, without either hiding the changes or
     making the whole sequence uninterpretable?


BOTTLENECK 5 - Cold start of the symbolic channel

The symbolic channel applies an inherited somatic scale only when the
heir's own experience log is non-empty. Newly born heirs have an empty log.
Therefore the first decision of a new generation systematically ignores the
inherited data, and in the first heir generation the somatic half of
inheritance is structurally zero.

The episodic half does transfer: heirs receive selected records from the
parent. But the scale factor, which is the part that modulates how those
records are weighted, does not apply until the heir has accumulated its own
history.

QUESTIONS
 5a. How do recent multi-generational agent frameworks initialise an
     inherited internal state at t = 0 of a new lifetime, without hard
     coding the state?
 5b. What distillation, summarisation, or latent-mapping methods let a
     newborn agent inherit a meaningful prior derived from the parent's
     lived experience, in a way that is verifiably NOT an injected trait?
     The distinction between "compressed history" and "assigned
     disposition" is exactly what our axiom rests on, so we need it drawn
     sharply.
 5c. Is there a principled reason to WANT a cold start, i.e. is the empty
     initial state a feature that preserves the claim that traits emerge
     from living rather than being given?
 5d. If both channels should be seeded at birth, how is the relative weight
     of the symbolic and parametric channel determined without tuning it
     against the outcome?
