-----------------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT THIS BRIEF IS NOT ASKING
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

We are NOT asking how large our effect is, or how large it is likely to be,
or what effect size is typical in our field. We deliberately withhold our own
measured numbers. Any answer that estimates our effect for us is unusable to
us, because our protocol forbids choosing a threshold after looking at an
outcome.

We are asking a METHODS question: given a statistic and a budget, how is the
sample size justified and how is the resulting insensitivity declared.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY THIS BRIEF EXISTS, GIVEN THAT WE ALREADY ASKED SOMETHING SIMILAR
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

We previously asked how to justify a smallest effect size of interest
(SESOI). The answer we received and adopted was: do NOT declare a SESOI when
no reference effect exists in the literature for your endpoint; instead use a
budget-constrained sample-size justification plus a sensitivity power
analysis, and report the minimum detectable effect (MDE) rather than claiming
absence of effect. The source given was Lakens (2022), "Sample Size
Justification", Collabra: Psychology 8(1):33267, doi 10.1525/collabra.33267.
We verified that source and it is real and correctly described. We used it,
and it worked: our primary endpoint at the time was a paired per-seed
difference tested with a Wilcoxon signed-rank test, and a sensitivity
analysis gave us a clean MDE in d_z units.

That machinery no longer applies, and this is the whole reason for this
brief. Our statistic has changed. It is now a COVARIANCE between reproductive
success and a trait value, computed inside a population, once per generation
transition -- the selection term of the Price equation. We do not know how to
run a sensitivity analysis on that, and we could not find one.

Two failure modes from that earlier round that we would like to avoid
repeating, both of which were caused partly by our own brief:

(a) We wrote that our runs are bit-level deterministic. The answer inferred
    from this that the correlation between paired arms must be very high
    (r >= 0.85), and used that to argue our design was more sensitive than it
    looked. That inference was invalid: determinism means that re-running the
    SAME arm gives the same bytes. It says nothing about the correlation
    between DIFFERENT arms across seeds. Please do not make this inference.

(b) The answer gave a range of effect sizes said to be typical for studies of
    internal representations, with no citation attached. We could not use it.
    Every quantitative claim in your answer must carry a source.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE DESIGN, DESCRIBED HONESTLY
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

This is a simulation study of whether experience acquired by an artificial
agent during its life leaves a trace that is transmitted to its offspring,
and whether that trace is subject to selection.

Agents. Each agent is a local Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct model in 4-bit
quantization with its own LoRA adapter. One decision per event.

Life. An agent lives a fixed number of events in a shared common-pool
resource environment with scarcity and crisis pressure, a memory store with
forgetting, and a prediction-error signal computed from sentence-embedding
distance. There is no human labelling and no model-as-judge anywhere.

Population. Each experimental arm has its own population of 8 agents and its
own separate resource pool. Population size is fixed: when an agent dies, a
replacement heir is produced from the winner of a tournament of size 2, so
the number of offspring per parent, w, takes values 0, 1, 2, and so on. The
run covers 3 generations, which yields 2 generation transitions per arm per
seed.

Arms. Three arms per seed, paired within seed by construction:
  - lived: the adapter is trained on preference pairs derived from the
    agent's own lived events
  - shuffle: the same pairs with the preference direction reversed
  - null: no training at all
The primary contrast is lived versus shuffle. null is descriptive only.

Endpoint. The trait z is a drift magnitude read at a FIXED event ordinal in
each life, so that lives of unequal length are compared at the same age. It
is a THRESHOLDED quantity: it is written only when an event's magnitude
crosses a fixed severity threshold. In many cells no agent crosses it, so z
has zero within-cell variance in those cells.

Statistic. Per generation transition, the selection term of the Price
equation, i.e. the covariance across the 8 agents of the population between w
(number of heirs) and z (the trait). Replicated over independent seeds.

Determinism. Same seed plus same code gives bit-identical results. Different
seeds give different results. Seeds are the only source of variance. Please
read this exactly as written and see section 1(a).

Cost. Measured, not estimated: about 2 hours of GPU time per seed, with a
spread of about 2.3x depending on the environment the founders are born into.
The run is a single shot: once the pre-registration is locked we cannot add
seeds afterwards, because that would make the analysis post-hoc.

3. CONSTRAINTS THAT CANNOT BE RELAXED
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Please do not propose anything that requires breaking these. If you believe a
constraint should be broken, say so explicitly and separately, and say why.

C1. No trait injection. No personality or trait value is ever assigned to an
    agent. Traits may only emerge from lived events.
C2. All metrics are deterministic code. No model is used as a judge.
C3. No wall-clock time. Only event ordering.
C4. Constants may not be tuned by looking at an outcome. A constant may be
    chosen from the literature, or derived from other constants by an
    inequality, but never selected because it made a result come out.
C5. Reproducibility requirement. The same seed with the same code must give
    the same result. NOTE: this does not mean we run one seed. We have
    previously run 40 seeds. Repeating a seed must reproduce; using many
    seeds is expected and required.
C6. No post-hoc protocol change. Everything is locked before the run.
C7. Null and underpowered results are legitimate outputs and are published as
    such. We are not looking for a way to get a positive result.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. QUESTIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS FOR A COVARIANCE STATISTIC.

Is there an established way to perform a sensitivity or minimum-detectable-
effect analysis when the primary statistic is a covariance between two
variables measured within a small group, replicated over independent runs?
What is the standard effect-size metric for such a statistic, and what is the
standard way to report "we were only powered to detect covariances at least
this large"? If the established practice is instead to simulate the null by
permutation and report the detectable region empirically, say so and cite
where that practice is described.

Q2. WHICH SAMPLE SIZE IS "THE" SAMPLE SIZE.

Our design has three nested counts: the number of independent seeds, the
number of agents inside one population (8), and the number of generation
transitions per seed (2). Statistical power presumably does not depend on all
three the same way. Which of these is the replication unit for a covariance
statistic of this kind, and what happens to the other two? Specifically: is
the covariance over 8 individuals best treated as one noisy observation per
transition, or as an estimate with its own internal standard error that
should be propagated?

Q3. THE SMALL-N BIAS OF THE PRICE ESTIMATOR.

We are aware of Rice (2008), "A stochastic version of the Price equation
reveals the interplay of deterministic and stochastic processes in
evolution", BMC Evolutionary Biology 8:262, doi 10.1186/1471-2148-8-262,
which indicates that in very small populations the expected selection
differential departs systematically from the classical deterministic
expectation, and that this departure shrinks as population size grows. We
have already recorded this as a declared limitation.

Our question is narrower: does this bias interact with a power or sensitivity
analysis, and if so how? Concretely, if the estimator is biased away from
zero at small N, is a sensitivity analysis computed on the unbiased
assumption optimistic, pessimistic, or neither? Is there a recommended
correction, or a recommended way to report the covariance so that the bias is
visible rather than absorbed?

Q4. A THRESHOLDED ENDPOINT WITH MANY ZERO-VARIANCE CELLS.

Our trait z is written only when a severity threshold is crossed. In many
cells of the design, no member of the population crosses it, so z is constant
within that cell and the covariance is exactly zero -- not because nothing
was selected, but because the endpoint recorded nothing to select on.

How is this situation handled in the methodological literature? Is there an
accepted name for a cell in which the endpoint is undefined rather than null?
How should such cells enter, or be excluded from, a sample-size or
sensitivity calculation, and how should their proportion be reported so that
a reader cannot mistake "undefined" for "zero"? We would rather change how we
REPORT than change the threshold, because changing the threshold after seeing
which cells are empty would violate C4.

Q5. IS THE BUDGET-CONSTRAINED FRAME STILL CORRECT HERE.

Given Q1 through Q4, is "choose the number of seeds from the compute budget,
then declare the minimum detectable effect" still the defensible frame for a
covariance statistic, as it was for our earlier paired-difference statistic?
If yes, please give the exact wording pattern a paper should use so that a
reader cannot confuse insensitivity with absence. If no, say what replaces
it.

Q6. PRIOR ART.

Are there published simulation or agent-based studies that report a Price
equation decomposition together with an explicit power, sensitivity, or
detectability statement? We are specifically looking for a worked example we
can imitate, not for a theoretical treatment of the Price equation. If none
exists, say so explicitly -- see section 5.
